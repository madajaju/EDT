The Real Problem Behind “More Data”

Data only matters when it changes how people decide. That’s the core lesson from this conversation with Sebastian Vinicky, author of Data Inspired, and it’s one technologist and business leaders can’t afford to ignore.

Most digital transformation programs start with a promise: better decisions, smarter automation, and stronger performance. But too often, organizations end up with more dashboards, more noise, and the same old behaviors.

Why Dashboards Don’t Create Change

Data is not a reporting layer

A common mistake in digital transformation is treating data like a scoreboard. Teams collect metrics, build dashboards, and call it progress — but reporting alone does not create better outcomes.

If people use numbers to defend their position instead of question it, the culture hasn’t changed. The data may be visible, but it isn’t influencing behavior.

Leadership sets the tone

Real change starts with leadership. Leaders decide whether data will be used to reinforce assumptions or challenge them.

That means setting expectations for curiosity, accountability, and honesty. If employees are rewarded only for looking good, they’ll hide uncertainty. If they’re rewarded for learning, they’ll surface it.

Data-Inspired vs. Data-Driven

The goal is better decisions, not automated thinking

“Data-driven” sounds strong, but taken literally, it suggests the data should make the decision. That’s not realistic in business, where most decisions are messy, high-stakes, and shaped by context.

A better model is data-inspired decision making. Data should help leaders test options, explore scenarios, and see blind spots — not replace judgment.

AI amplifies the culture you already have

AI won’t fix weak decision-making. It will speed it up.

If your organization already rushes to conclusions, AI will make that habit more efficient. If your culture values thoughtful analysis, AI can become a powerful tool for strategy, forecasting, and innovation.

Key takeaways

  • Use data to explore options, not just validate opinions.

  • Build incentives that reward curiosity and transparency.

  • Keep humans accountable for strategic decisions.

  • Treat AI as an assistant, not an oracle.

What Leaders Should Do Next

Build a decision culture, not just a data stack

The winners in the next phase of digital transformation won’t be the companies with the most tools. They’ll be the ones with the clearest decision architecture — who decides, with what inputs, and why.

Start by asking: Are our meetings encouraging challenge or compliance? Are we using analytics to improve the business, or to protect the status quo?

Make data useful, not overwhelming

More data is not automatically better. Better framing is. When teams understand the business problem first, data becomes a sharper tool for solving it.

That’s especially important as AI adoption accelerates. The real edge will come from leaders who can combine machine speed with human judgment.
