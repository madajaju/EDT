Episode 388 AI Literacy and the Hidden Power of Digital Persuasion

2026-09-24 Hosted by Dr. Darren Pulsipher

Explore more in the episode archive.

Summary

AI is reshaping not just what we see, but how we think, trust, and decide. Host Dr. Darren sits down with Diego Bonilla, CSU professor and AI literacy expert, to unpack digital persuasion, privacy, critical thinking, and how AI can move from personalized influence toward collective intelligence. Key

Diego Bonilla Darren W Pulsipher

A new kind of influence problem

AI is no longer just a productivity tool or content engine. It is shaping attention, trust, privacy, and decision-making in ways most people do not fully see yet.

Diego Bonilla’s perspective is especially relevant for technologists and business leaders because it connects AI literacy, media literacy, and persuasion into one urgent conversation. The real question is not whether AI can generate text or images, but whether it can quietly shape what people believe, buy, and support.

Critical thinking is still the core skill

The foundation every medium depends on

One of the strongest ideas here is simple: critical thinking is the baseline skill across every medium. Whether the platform is television, social media, or generative AI, people need the ability to question what they are seeing and why it is being shown to them.

That matters because digital systems are built to collect signals. Clicks, searches, pauses, and preferences can all be used to infer behavior and personalize influence. In plain terms, the system learns how to persuade you by learning who you are.

Why literacy has become a business issue

For organizations, this is not just a social concern. It is a leadership issue tied to risk, privacy, and judgment.

If teams rely on a single platform, a single model, or a single channel, they become easier to influence and harder to protect. That is why AI literacy should sit alongside cybersecurity, governance, and digital strategy.

Key takeaways

  • Teach critical thinking as a workplace skill, not just an academic one.

  • Audit where your organization depends on one vendor or model.

  • Treat privacy and persuasion as operational risks.

  • Ask what the system is optimizing: truth, engagement, or behavior change?

From broadcast media to personalized persuasion

Why the feed feels so powerful

Traditional media broadcast the same message to many people. Today’s platforms do the opposite: they narrowcast, tailoring content to each user. That creates echo chambers, increases cognitive overload, and makes it harder for people to compare notes with one another.

Advertising plays a huge role here. When a platform is “free,” the product is often the user’s attention. That means the platform is incentivized to keep people hooked, not necessarily informed.

AI makes the problem more intimate

Generative AI raises the stakes because it can feel like a collaborator, coach, or companion. That makes persuasion more personal and more invisible.

The most important shift may not be the content AI creates, but the interactions it enables. If AI knows your habits, context, and preferences, it can shape responses in ways that feel helpful while still nudging your decisions.

A better future is collective, not isolated

Designing AI for shared intelligence

There is another path. Instead of using AI only for individual consumption, leaders can design systems that help people reason together.

Imagine a communal workspace where questions, insights, and patterns are shared across a group rather than trapped in private silos. That kind of design supports dialogue, collective intelligence, and better decision-making.

What leaders should do now

The opportunity is not just to use AI faster. It is to use AI more wisely.

Build guardrails. Strengthen media literacy. Encourage human conversation. And choose tools that expand judgment instead of replacing it.
