Why Outages Are a Leadership Test

A technical outage can quickly turn into a business crisis, especially when every minute costs real money. That’s why cloud war games, AWS training, and cybersecurity simulations matter for today’s leaders.

Matt Lea from Schematic AI emphasizes that outage response is about more than fixing systems; it’s about teams thinking clearly, communicating effectively, and recovering under pressure, which keeps leaders engaged.

What Simulated Pressure Reveals

Scenario-based training surfaces the gaps passive learning misses

Most teams know the theory, but theory doesn’t show what happens when people freeze. Simulated outages expose single points of failure, weak communication, and the knowledge that lives in just one person’s head.

That makes this kind of training especially relevant for CTOs, CIOs, cloud teams, and security leaders, as it demonstrates how simulations prepare teams for real cloud outages or attacks beyond slide decks and modules.

Key takeaways

  • Passive training checks a box; simulations build readiness.

  • Outage drills reveal how teams behave under stress.

  • Cross-training reduces dependence on one expert.

War games also double as a hiring signal

One of the smartest parts of this approach is that it can be used as an interviewing tool. In a live scenario, it becomes obvious who collaborates, who asks good questions, and who shuts down.

That matters more now that AI tools can help candidates answer standard interview questions. A realistic pressure test shows how someone thinks, not just what they can recite.

Gamifying Cloud and Security Learning

Turning infrastructure into something people can actually grasp

Matt is also building an indie game called Tech Debt, designed to teach cloud principles and basic cybersecurity engagingly. Instead of abstract jargon, players learn through familiar game mechanics like leveling up, protecting systems, and responding to attacks.

That’s a smart move for teams and younger learners entering tech. Concepts like firewalls, phishing attacks, and database load are easier to understand when they’re part of a hands-on experience.

Why this approach fits the future of tech training

Gamification works because it creates feedback loops. People see the impact of decisions, make mistakes in a safe environment, and remember the lesson when it matters in real life.

It also helps business leaders think about resilience differently. Cloud cost, security, and operational readiness are all connected, and training should reflect that reality.

Building Teams That Don’t Collapse Under Pressure

The biggest lesson here is simple: resilience is a leadership skill, not just an engineering task. Companies can buy more tools and cloud capacity, but they still fail if knowledge is trapped in one person or teams can’t act under stress.

If you manage technical teams, ask this: could your organization recover if your top expert were unavailable tomorrow? If the answer is no, it’s time to rehearse failure before failure rehearses you.
